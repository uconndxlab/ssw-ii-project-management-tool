# AI design smells in operational web apps

On November 24, 2026, I searched for recurring patterns in generated interfaces, documented generator defaults, and design guidance for admin work. I favored 2025–2026 sources and kept older guidance where it addressed a specific task. Vendor docs establish starting configurations. First-hand examples and public anti-pattern repositories show what people recognize. Design systems and usability research explain what makes an alternative work.

The strongest evidence concerns repeated visual defaults. Carter Johnson analyzed 3,033 comments from 125 selected threads about AI-built sites. The corpus measures what people mention in those threads. It cannot estimate how often a pattern appears in generated admin apps. Leo Stehlik's internal review-queue demo gives a concrete comparison, with one worked example. The operational rows draw on design guidance. Their explanations are inferences about why a screen feels templated, and a screen's appearance cannot establish who built it. [Corpus methodology](https://github.com/JCarterJohnson/vibecoded-design-tells/blob/main/README.md), [Review-queue comparison](https://github.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui/blob/master/docs/conversion-proof.md)

## Catalog

## Not in impeccable's catalog

Compared against all 62 local detector rules, the local craft floor, and [the repo audit's §9](design-audit.md#9-ai-design-smells-2026-11-24). A gap here means the catalog lacks the specific review case. Its broad guidance still applies. For example, it asks for loading, error, and empty states but leaves their behavior to the designer.

The 10 gaps below are ranked by their impact on recurring work and recovery.

1. **Setup versus progress.** Give missing assignees or targets their own labeled location. Keep urgency visible without displaying a setup issue as a progress measurement.
2. **One empty message for different causes.** Staff need to know whether to add, clear filters, request access, or retry. Section 9 only mentions an empty message that duplicates a zero badge.
3. **Validation that hides the fix.** Keep entered values and take users to the field that needs correction. The craft floor's copy rule covers naming the recovery, while this check covers retaining input and locating the error.
4. **Invisible filter state.** Make a subset obvious so a filtered queue cannot be mistaken for the whole queue.
5. **Ambiguous bulk selection.** State whether a bulk change affects one page or all matching records.
6. **Important row actions hidden in ellipses.** Keep the routine operation visible where people scan the row.
7. **Cards where users compare rows.** Judge the layout by cross-record comparison. The existing card rule focuses on repeated card structure.
8. **Charts without a question.** Require a business question and an interpretable measure. The existing guidance calls out ornamental sparklines and rings.
9. **Tooltips as a second interface.** Put required information in view. The detector's tooltip clipping rule addresses rendering only.
10. **Skeletons and spinners as decoration.** Match the scope and duration of the loading indicator to the request. A generic loading-state checklist misses this.

Other gaps in the detector and craft floor include bento sizing unrelated to work order, shipping the entire stock component theme, applying icons to every control, and button copy that stays generic even when it meets the craft floor's instruction to name the action. Section 9 adds examples of greetings, success-toast titles, title and field filler, zero badges, and repeated status dots. The detector includes a pulsing-dot rule, which addresses motion. The craft floor already covers nested cards, metric heroes, decorative glass, pills, unnecessary modals, over-animation, and ornamental sparklines.

## Disputed or weak

- **Bento, glass, and mesh backgrounds.** Johnson found few mentions of bento or glass in the comment sample and dropped mesh results after checking false matches. Stehlik treats glass panels as a practical warning in an internal queue, while Apple uses translucency to distinguish functional chrome from content. Judge what the treatment does in that screen. [Johnson's tell catalog](https://github.com/JCarterJohnson/vibecoded-design-tells/blob/main/skill/references/tells.md), [No Slop UI patterns](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui/master/references/banned-patterns.md), [Apple materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- **Fonts and the component stack.** v0 documents a Next.js, shadcn/ui, and Tailwind starting point. Bolt documents a template with shadcn/ui and Lucide. Lovable documents how to bring a custom system. The evidence covers specific workflows and leaves cross-tool comparisons open. [v0 design systems](https://v0.app/docs/design-systems-2), [Bolt project settings](https://support.bolt.new/building/using-bolt/project-settings), [Lovable design systems](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/design-systems)
- **Status dots, stripes, and redundant cues.** A decorative dot that repeats the tint differs from a useful symbol that can be recognized without color. A stroke can show hierarchy or indicate selection. Preserve the information and make each cue earn its place. [Carbon status indicators](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/patterns/status-indicators), [GOV.UK tags](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/tag/), [Local audit §9](design-audit.md#9-ai-design-smells-2026-11-24)
- **Greetings, exclamation marks, and em dashes.** The local greeting and toast are concrete examples. Atlassian also allows celebration for infrequent milestones. Graphite's prose tests find variation in dash frequency by model and version. A single greeting or punctuation mark carries little information about authorship. [Atlassian success messages](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/designing-messages/success-messages/), [Graphite model comparison](https://graphite.io/research/ai-tells)
- **Short empty states, sparklines, and motion can do real work.** “No assignments yet” can fit a genuinely empty list with an obvious next step. Tufte demonstrates how a tiny trend supplies context. Atlassian uses motion to clarify state and guide attention. The warning sign is repetition with no job to do. [Carbon empty states](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/patterns/empty-states), [Edward Tufte on sparklines](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/), [Atlassian motion](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion/applying-motion)
- **Broad bans have weak support.** No Slop UI disallows several aesthetic choices, such as mixed serif and sans type or routine table striping. Those choices depend on the content and design system. Anthropic's landing-page report describes minimal motion as a default, so use excessive motion as a prompt to review whether it helps users. [No Slop UI patterns](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LeoStehlik/no-slop-ui/master/references/banned-patterns.md), [Anthropic frontend skills](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
